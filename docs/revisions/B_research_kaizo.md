# Kaizo Trap: craft notes for the chase restage

**Source and method.** Watched directly: "孔明の罠 - Kaizo Trap" (Guy Collins Animation, YouTube `lIES3ii-IOg`, 8:12, 25 fps) at 360p, as 2 fps contact sheets of the whole film, 6–8 fps sheets of the action, and frame-by-frame strips of single jumps. Cut times come from ffmpeg scene detection; tempo (130 BPM, 0.46 s per beat) from onset analysis of the audio, which I did not hear. Frame counts are approximate at 360p, and 5:00–8:12 was only skimmed at 0.5 fps. Timestamps are video time.

## Movement grammar

- **Few poses, snapped.** Stand, sprint-lean, rise, fall, dive. No acceleration ramp: she lands upright at 2:25.60 and is in a full 35° forward lean with arms thrown back by 2:25.72.
- **Short hop** (1:52.64–1:53.36): 2 frames of crouch, about 15 frames airborne (0.6 s), settled 2 frames after touchdown. No deep squash.
- **Long gap** (2:02.24–2:03.24): about 25 frames airborne. At 2:02.68, just past the apex, she snaps to a tucked dive pose; she lands prone and skids for 4 frames (2:03.28–2:03.44) before popping upright.
- **The ponytail is the speedometer.** It streams straight back in a sprint (2:25.72–2:26.76), goes level at a jump's apex (1:52.96), and flips up on the fall (1:53.16–1:53.28). The body barely deforms.
- **Speed is not drawn on her.** No smears or speed lines in gameplay (speed lines appear only in the prologue, 1:11–1:16). Speed comes from background scroll and parallax, the trailing hair, and her gaining on the camera.
- **Contact marker.** Each stomp is a 1-frame white disc (2:50.56), then a grey dust ring that stays in world space for about 10 frames and slides off behind her (2:50.64–2:51.00).

## Camera

- The prologue (0:23–1:35) is cinematic: cuts, close-ups, 3/4 angles. From 1:50.5 it is flat side-on with her about 1/7 of frame height.
- **One continuous take per world.** Cuts fall only on world changes: 2:08.5, 2:15.88, 2:30.64, 2:52.80, 3:00.20.
- The camera follows on both axes and tilts down ahead of a fall (1:55.4–1:55.9). In sprints it trails, so she drifts from x≈0.38 to 0.55 of frame (2:25.7–2:26.8).
- **World changes cut on her airborne pose**, at nearly the same screen position (2:15.88, 2:52.80, 4:17.72).
- It breaks the 2D plane only for emotion: the annoyed close-up after the first death (1:59.48–2:01.16), the boss reveal (3:33–3:40), the punch (4:43.2).

## Near-misses and telegraphing

- **Fail, then the same jump made** (1:54.56 → 2:02.24). Attempt 1 is a plain jump: the far bank slides past above her at top right (1:55.4–1:55.6), the camera tilts, spikes appear only 6 frames before impact (1:55.76 → 1:56.00). Attempt 2 uses the same gap and framing with the dive added. Because we saw the short arc, the dive reads as the save.
- **Hide the outcome behind the hazard.** The boss beam kills her at 3:40.76. At 4:30.40 the framing is repeated exactly; the beam fills the frame for about 1.1 s (4:30.56–4:31.6), then a cut wide shows her above it (4:31.68).
- **The pursuer lunges where she was.** The cat pounces as she bounces off a bird and passes under her into the pit (2:50.72–2:51.04).
- **Telegraphs.** Enemies enter from a frame edge 8–10 frames before contact (bird 2:50.20 → 2:50.56). Lethal surfaces carry old blood (crusher 2:25.9–2:26.8, fortress platforms 3:04–3:09). Spikes are white combs with red tips. Arrows are rows of small glyphs (2:21.6–2:23.0).

## Direction and geography

- Left to right, always, with vertical detours. She faces right in almost every gameplay frame.
- The goal is planted screen-right in the prologue: the TV is on the right and she is dragged rightward into it (1:04–1:16). The boss stands at the right (4:26–4:31).
- Static hazards sit ahead or below. Birds fly right to left against her. Projectiles from behind overtake her and stick in the wall ahead (arrows, 2:21.6–2:23.0).
- The one true pursuer, the cat (2:45.5–2:51.0), crosses the frame both ways while her heading never changes. She never turns toward it.

## Death, retry, escalation, and the music lock

- **Every world lasts 7.36 s, which is 16 beats (4 bars)**: 2:01.16, 2:08.52, 2:15.88, 2:23.2 (a scroll, not a cut), 2:30.64, then 2:45.44, 2:52.80, 3:00.20.
- First attempt is slow and hesitant (walks 1:50.6–1:52.5, pauses 1:53.4–1:54.5) and dies on a beat (1:56.00). The corpse blinks twice, she drops back in at spawn (1:58), then one bar of face.
- **The retry skips what we have seen**: it starts mid-level (2:01.16) and reaches the gap in 1.1 s.
- **Death montage** (2:30.64–2:45.44, 8 bars, about 50 cuts): one death every 2 beats, then every beat (from 2:35.24), half-beat (about 2:38), quarter-beat at 3 frames a shot (2:41.7–2:45.4). It mixes worlds already passed with worlds not yet seen (cat 2:36.6, lava 2:41.16), so resuming far ahead at 2:45.44 feels earned.
- Hazard density climbs from one at a time (2:01–2:08) to 6–10 sawblades on screen (3:19–3:25). The palette darkens to near-black; her red and purple never change.
- **Reprise at double speed** (4:14–4:26): the late worlds get 2 bars each (cuts 4:17.72, 4:21.44) in storm weather. The boss then cycles about nine distinct attack shapes in 11 s (4:31.7–4:42.6).

## Game UI

- No HUD at all: no lives, timer, or score.
- A diegetic manual (1:41–1:46) teaches the moveset with sprite and button diagrams before she uses it.
- "CONTINUE?" ghosts in over the montage (2:41.7–2:45.4), then becomes a physical scene with glowing Y and N doors and a 10-to-7 countdown that ticks about once per bar (3:52–4:06).
- Corpse blink and sky-drop respawn (1:57–1:58), "TOOL-ASSIST PROHIBITED" signage (3:32), a DOS boot flash (4:12.7–4:13.7).

## Second-hand

Nerdist's write-up says the short reflects Collins's interest in speedruns and that the "Continue?" prompt leads to paid secret-ending material. The YouTube description names the track as Leslie Wai's "Paradigm". I found no making-of interview or production notes; nothing above depends on one.

## What to steal for a 47-second chase

1. **Bar-length locations.** One location per 4 bars as a single side-on take, cutting only on bar lines and on her airborne pose. Drop to 2 bars per location for the last third.
2. **Lock left to right and plant the goal screen-right.** Agents enter from the left edge behind her or from above. Anything moving right to left is an obstacle, never something she runs at.
3. **Stage the chasm dash as a fail and retry pair.** Show the short arc with the far beam passing above her, rewind, then repeat the takeoff and framing with a pose-snap dash just past the apex and a 4-frame skid landing.
4. **Hide her behind the volley.** Let the agents' fire fill the frame where she stood for about a second, then cut wide to find her above it.
5. **Gunfire as glyph rows that overtake her** from behind and stick in the wall ahead, entering frame 8–10 frames before they pass.
6. **Pursuers lunge at where she was** and overshoot within the same shot, so she never has to turn.
7. **A trailing element as the speedometer** (jacket hem, loose hair): back in a sprint, level at the apex, up on the fall. Snap between four or five poses with a 2-frame anticipation.
8. **Leave contact marks in world space**: a 1-frame white disc plus a 10-frame dust ring at every dash, landing, and bullet strike.
9. **Accelerating montage** for the multiplied Jades or repeated attempts: 2 beats, 1, half, quarter, then a hard cut on a downbeat into a long clean take.
10. **No HUD.** Save UI for one diegetic stakes beat, ticking once per bar.
