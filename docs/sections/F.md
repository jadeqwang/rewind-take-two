# Section F: final drop + outro (169.40–227.96)

Director: section F agent. Status: **done and cut.** Every F shot is generated, colour-passed and placed in
`media/shots/F_edl.json` (engine EDL fallback, generated by `tools/sectionF/edl.py`). O1 is section E's 3D shot (in
`render/timeline/E.js`). F only adds O1's variance-report overlay. Preview of the whole section with audio:
`node render/engine/render.mjs --timeline tools/sectionF/F_preview_timeline.js --range 169.4 227.96 --preview --encode --force`
(this loads only F's EDL plus E's O1, so a broken section elsewhere can't block it). The full film (`node render/engine/render.mjs --list`)
loads F with no overlap errors. Stills: `docs/sections/F_media/` (style test, F1 flood frames, contact sheet of the cut).

## The look: "the same drawing, now blooming"

Style test (`media/shots/F_style/sheet_style_r1.jpg`): 4 keyframes from the same prompt on two models, nano-banana-pro
and gpt-image-2.5-flare. The refs were the hair-down sheet, the canon turnaround and an engraved world plate.

- **nano-banana-pro won** and is the house model for every F keyframe. The black engraved hatching stays on top, the
  colored pencil is laid *into* the hatching, and the sky stays warm paper with a few Kandinsky shapes and gold spirals.
  It reads as the B&W world with colour arriving.
- gpt-image-2.5-flare made a maximal pop collage (mosaic skies, checkerboards everywhere). Gorgeous, but a different
  film, and it doesn't read in under a second. Rejected.
- Engrave pass (`F_style/sheet_engrave_test.jpg`): `tools/engrave` with **no bloom** turns a colour frame into a
  convincing B&W engraving *of the same image*. That's how the F1 "before" plate is made. Partial engraving over the
  colour footage (strength 0.3–0.5 with full bloom) only added gray blotches and muddied the paper. **Rule:** colour
  shots go through `tools/engrave` at strength 0 with full bloom (anime upscale to 1080p plus Jade's matte, colour
  untouched), via `tools/sectionF/colorpass.sh`. Nano already drew the hatching into them.
- **Inversion rule:** the agents stay pure black-ink engraving inside the colour world. At the start of the film Jade
  was the only colour; now the agents are the only uncoloured things. That's the "it couldn't trace" payoff.
- **Colour arc:** F1 engraved → colour; F2–F4 bright colored pencil on paper; F5–F6 full colour plus Klimt gold (the
  boss); F7 the maximal gold-leaf sky (BIG 1); F8 calm; O2 soft dawn.
- Jade's hair is down and she holds the pencil throughout (ref `media/design/takes/jade_pencil_flare_t2.png` + canon).
  In O2 she twists her hair up and pushes the pencil back in (New Game+).

## F1: the colour flood (matte wipe)

`tools/sectionF/wipe.py` composites the engraved twin (`take_3_twin_engraved.mp4`, from `tools/engrave` strength 1, no
bloom) and the colour pass of the **same clip** through an animated radial matte. The matte grows from her (she *is* the
pencil point), with an fbm-wobbled, hatched front and a gold-leaf leading edge. It starts on the impact (169.40) and
reaches the corners on the bar-98 downbeat (171.18). Output: `media/shots/F1/take_3_wipe.mp4` plus
`take_3_wipe_matte.mp4` (white = colour), so the engine can redo the wipe itself if wanted. The same tool with
`--reverse` makes F7_08, **the rewind that paints**: F6c played backward under ◀◀ while the colour paints *in*.
Note for tools/engrave: its new `--bloom` (pencil mode) arrived after this was built. My wipe is a plain composite of
two engrave outputs and doesn't depend on it.

## What's weak

- **Engine ghosts were dropped**: on F6c take 1 the engine's `ghosts` layer smeared, because the tools/engrave matte
  carries patches of background. F6c was regenerated with the ghosts drawn into the keyframe (take 2, crisp). Anyone
  else relying on matte-driven ghosts should expect the same smear.
- **Seedance softening**: over 5 s some takes drift from hatched pencil towards smoother painted colour (F6c, F2's
  sky). I only use the first 1–3.5 s of most takes, which hides most of it.
- **The O2 notice track** is hand-keyed from 6 frames. It holds well until the door swings (the last 0.4 s), where it
  approximates the perspective.
- The F2b/F3 "never stop" positions are chosen by eye on moving plates (F3 rolls continuously). They're readable but
  not tracked to a surface.
- The O1 overlay assumes section E's O1 framing. The report panel sits bottom-right, which is clear of the diorama in
  E's pull-back.
